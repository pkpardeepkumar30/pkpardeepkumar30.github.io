I trained as an engineer, spent more than ten years on hard technical problems in two countries, and have arrived at the conviction that the most useful thing I can do next is in school education.

## A path across disciplines and borders

I trained in aerospace engineering at IIT Bombay, completing my MSc in 2014. My career began in India, building simulation and engineering software, and later took me to the Netherlands.

Over more than ten years, I have worked across energy, semiconductors, engineering software, finance, and scientific research. That journey includes Fluidyn, Altair, KLA-Tencor, Aakraya Research, Shell, and ASM International.

My doctoral research at CWI Amsterdam and TU Delft focused on scientific computing and numerical methods. My thesis has been submitted and approved, and the defence is scheduled for January 2027 at TU Delft. This work strengthened habits I value in any endeavour: making assumptions explicit, testing ideas carefully, and following through on difficult problems.

## From ideas to execution

I enjoy building things that people can use. Alongside my engineering work, I have independently designed and developed digital products, including a civic strategy simulation, an Indian news platform, a multiplayer chess application, and a project workspace.

Those projects come from the same instinct that draws me to education: understand a need, think carefully about the experience of the person on the other side, and do the work to make an idea actually useful.

If you want the technical detail, it lives on my background pages: [engineering](../software/), [research](../research/), [publications](../publications/), [talks](../talks/), and [independent products](../web/).

## Why education

Mathematics, physics, and computation have been central to my own learning. I know how much the right explanation, example, or question can change someone's understanding of a difficult subject. I also know the opposite: how quickly a child decides a subject is "not for them" when nobody takes the time to ask what they are actually confused about.

Research trains you to be comfortable not knowing. You sit with a problem, you keep asking why, you accept being wrong in public, and eventually something opens up. I have never understood why that way of working is treated as an advanced skill reserved for graduate students, rather than the ordinary way a curious ten-year-old could be taught.

What I care about is that curiosity and real understanding stop being things that follow family income. [That conviction is set out in full here](../what-i-believe/).

## Beyond work

I enjoy ping-pong, cooking Indian food, and exploring Indian philosophy. These interests keep me curious about the ways people learn, think, and live.
